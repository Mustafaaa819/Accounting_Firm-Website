Most of the questions we have fielded this quarter come down to one thing:
whether the practical expedient can still be applied to leases signed before
the transition date.

It usually can — but only if the original term is documented well enough to
survive an audit, which is where most files fall down.

## The relief is real, and it is narrower than it reads

The expedient lets you carry a lease onto the balance sheet using the
liability you would have measured at transition, rather than reconstructing
the accounting as though the standard had always applied. That is a genuine
saving. On a portfolio of a dozen sites it is the difference between a week of
work and a month of it.

The catch is in what "the lease as it stood at transition" means. It is not
the lease you are currently paying. It is the lease as originally contracted,
plus every variation agreed since — and the variations are the part nobody
keeps in one place. Rent reviews live in the property manager's inbox. Break
notices live with the solicitor who served them. A three-month rent-free
period agreed by email in 2023 lives nowhere at all.

> If you cannot produce the original term and every amendment to it, the
> expedient is not available to you. Not because the relief has expired —
> because you cannot evidence the number it produces.

## Where files actually fail

Four things, in the order we find them:

- **Undocumented extensions.** A lease that rolled on past its contractual end
  by agreement, with nothing signed. The tenant treats it as continuing; the
  standard treats it as a new lease from the date the old term ended.
- **Service charges bundled into rent.** A single monthly figure covering rent,
  service charge and insurance has to be split before it can be discounted.
  Where the lease does not split it, someone has to, and defend the split.
- **Incentives amortised on the old basis.** Rent-free periods and fit-out
  contributions carried forward at their old carrying value, rather than
  folded into the transition measurement.
- **The discount rate.** Most tenants use the incremental borrowing rate
  because the rate implicit in the lease is not determinable. Fine — but the
  rate has to be specific to the term and the security of the individual
  lease, not one group rate applied across a portfolio with terms ranging from
  two years to fifteen.

## What to do now

Pull the lease file for every property before you look at a single number. If
the file is complete, the accounting is a morning's work per site. If it is
not, the gap is a legal and administrative problem, and it takes weeks to
close — which is the real reason to start it now rather than at year end.

We are happy to review a portfolio and tell you which files are ready and
which are not, before anyone starts measuring anything. It is usually an
afternoon, and it is the cheapest part of the whole exercise.
