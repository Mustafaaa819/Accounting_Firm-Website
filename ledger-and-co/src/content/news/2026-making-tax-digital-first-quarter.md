The first quarterly update under Making Tax Digital for Income Tax covers 6
April to 5 July and is due on **7 August** — three days after this was
written. We have now filed for around 140 sole traders and landlords, which is
enough to say something more useful than "it went fine".

It did mostly go fine. The software worked, the submissions went through, and
nobody has had a rejection they could not resolve the same day. But the
quarter was more work than we quoted for on roughly a third of files, and the
overrun came from the same four places every time.

## What a quarterly update is not

Worth stating plainly, because half the anxiety we have dealt with this
summer came from a misunderstanding of what is being submitted.

A quarterly update is a **summary of income and expenses by category**. It is
not a tax calculation, it is not a return, and it does not have to be right in
the sense that a return has to be right. Errors are corrected in a later
update or at the year-end finalisation, which is the submission that actually
matters. Nobody is going to be assessed on a July figure that turned out to be
provisional.

> The quarterly update is a reading from a meter, not a statement of account.
> Clients who understood that had a calm quarter. Clients who treated each
> update as a mini tax return spent four times as long on it and were no more
> accurate at the year end.

## The four things that cost the time

**Uncategorised bank feeds.** By some distance the biggest cause. A year of
transactions sitting in "uncategorised" is invisible under annual accounting —
we sorted it in one pass each January and nobody noticed. Under quarterly
reporting it surfaces every three months, and the sorting is now four small
jobs instead of one large one, done at a point where the bank narrative is
still fresh enough to be recognisable. That last part is a genuine
improvement; it just did not feel like one in July.

**Mixed personal and business accounts.** If business and private spending run
through one account, someone has to separate them four times a year rather
than once. There is no software answer to this. Open a second account. It is
the single highest-return thing a sole trader can do before the next quarter,
and it takes twenty minutes.

**Property clients with more than one business.** Self-employment and property
income are separate businesses for MTD purposes and need separate updates. A
landlord who is also a consultant files two, not one. Several people had
budgeted for half the work.

**Digital links.** Where figures move from a spreadsheet into the filing
software, the link has to be digital — a formula, an import, an API — and not
a number retyped by a person. Copy-and-paste out of a PDF supplier statement
breaks the requirement even when the figure is correct. This is the one
compliance point we found people had genuinely not heard of.

## Who this applies to now, and who is next

The current threshold catches qualifying income above £50,000 — and qualifying
income means gross turnover from self-employment plus gross rental income,
before any expenses. That gross-not-net point catches people out constantly. A
landlord with £55,000 of rent and £40,000 of mortgage interest and costs is in
scope, despite a profit that is nowhere near the threshold.

The threshold steps down over the next two years, so a good number of people
reading this who are out of scope today will be in scope for the April after
next. Our advice to them is not to wait: the clients who had an easy July were
the ones already keeping digital records for their own reasons, and the
difficulty was never the filing.

## What to do before the next one

The second update covers 6 July to 5 October and is due on **7 November**.
Between now and then:

- Clear the uncategorised queue monthly rather than quarterly. Fifteen minutes
  a month replaces a bad afternoon.
- Open a separate business account if you have not.
- Check whether you have two businesses rather than one.
- Find any place where a figure is being retyped rather than linked, and fix
  the link.

If your first update was harder than it should have been, tell us which part.
We are rebuilding the process for the clients where it overran, and we would
rather do that on the evidence than on a guess.
