Payroll legislation arrives in two forms: the changes that are announced
loudly and then move, and the changes that take effect quietly on the sixth of
April whether or not anyone briefed you. This year has produced a good example
of each.

If you run your own payroll, the short version is that your April run needs
three adjustments and none of the system rebuild you were told to budget for.

## Payrolling benefits in kind: deferred again

Mandatory payrolling of benefits in kind — the change that would have retired
the P11D for most employers — has moved back another year. It is now an April
2027 start.

Our advice has not changed, and it is not "do nothing for twelve months".
Payrolling benefits voluntarily is still open, it still works, and the
employers who moved early are the ones having an easy time of it. The
mandatory version will land eventually, and the difference between a firm that
has already run a year of payrolled benefits and one that has not is roughly
a quarter of preparation.

The practical case for moving now:

- Employees see the tax on a benefit in the month they receive it, rather than
  through a coding notice eighteen months later, which removes the single most
  common payroll query we handle.
- The data cleanup — which cars, which fuel, which medical schemes, which
  employees actually left in March — is the hard part, and it does not get
  easier by being done under a deadline.
- You still file a P11D(b) and still pay Class 1A, so the year-end job does not
  disappear. It just gets much smaller.

The one thing you cannot do is register mid-year. Voluntary payrolling has to
be registered before the start of the tax year it applies to, so a decision
made in June is a decision about April 2027.

## Employee hours reporting: not proceeding as drafted

The requirement to report detailed hours worked through RTI has been dropped
in the form it was consulted on. Employers who have already added hours
capture to their timesheet process have not wasted the work — the data is
useful, and something along these lines will return — but there is no filing
obligation attached to it this year.

## The three that did take effect

None of these were controversial, which is exactly why they get missed.

**Statutory rate uprating.** The National Living Wage and the statutory
payments — sick pay, and the family leave rates — all rose from the start of
the tax year. The trap is not the rate itself, it is the pay reference period:
an employee whose pay period straddles the uprating date takes the new rate
from the start of the *next* full period, not from the sixth. Applying it
mid-period is the most common error we correct in April files.

**Neonatal care leave and pay.** Now a day-one right, with its own statutory
payment and its own eligibility test, which is not the same test as shared
parental pay. If your payroll software has not prompted you to configure it,
that is not evidence you have no eligible employees.

**Employer NIC on lower earnings.** The threshold at which employer
contributions begin is materially lower than it was two years ago, and the
rate is higher. For employers with a large part-time workforce, this is no
longer a rounding difference. If your last cost model for a part-time hire was
built before April 2025, it is understating the true cost by a meaningful
margin.

> Recalculate the fully-loaded cost of a part-time role before you next
> recruit for one. The number most business owners are carrying in their head
> is two years out of date.

## What we would do before the end of the month

Run one payroll, then reconcile it line by line against March — not against
the software's own "changes applied" report, which tells you what it thinks it
did rather than what it did. Every year the discrepancies land in the same
three places: employees on the boundary of a statutory payment, employees who
changed hours in the last week of March, and benefits that were switched
between payrolled and P11D treatment.

And note the two dates that have not moved: **6 July** for P11D and P11D(b)
filing, and **22 July** for the Class 1A payment where you pay
electronically. Deferring mandatory payrolling deferred nothing about those.

If you would like us to run the reconciliation alongside your own, send us the
March and April reports and we will tell you what has moved and whether it
should have.
